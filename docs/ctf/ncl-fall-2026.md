# National Cyber League · Fall 2026

[Back to portfolio](../../README.md)

I participated in the National Cyber League (NCL) Individual and Team Games as part of BACS 3001, the foundational Portfolio Practicum at SANS Technology Institute. The games gave me a setting to practice independent problem-solving and team coordination alongside my professional penetration testing work. [SANS degree curriculum](https://www.sans.edu/cyber-security-programs/bachelors-degree)

## Participation highlights

- **Individual Game.** I worked independently and used the competition to practice researching unfamiliar topics, checking assumptions, and validating my reasoning before submitting answers.
- **Team Game.** We opened with a voice call on the first day, shared our availability, and kept findings in dedicated channels. When someone asked how a teammate reached a conclusion, the group could discuss the reasoning and learn an approach they could apply to a different problem.

## Reflection

Working through unfamiliar problems under time constraints reinforced the value of pausing to test an assumption before committing to a conclusion. In the Team Game, recording findings in a shared channel made it easier for teammates with different schedules to pick up the work and ask informed questions.

Next time, I would introduce a second-person review for uncertain Team Game submissions. One teammate would record the supporting evidence, and another would check the reasoning before submission. That gives the team a concrete way to improve accuracy and helps each reviewer learn how someone else approached the problem. I plan to keep competing to practice these habits and explore technical areas outside my day-to-day assessments.
